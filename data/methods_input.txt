METHODS AND MATERIALS

Patient Population and Data Collection
We conducted a retrospective analysis of the medical records of patients with spontaneous SAH who were treated at our institution from January 2013 to December 2018. The study was approved by the ethics committee of the State of Upper Austria (EK Nr: 1028/2019). The study enrolled individuals of both genders who presented with spontaneous SAH, diagnosed either through radiological imaging (such as computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging) or confirmed by positive results from lumbar puncture. Additionally, the availability of outcome data was required for inclusion. We excluded traumatic SAH and other vascular malformations except cerebral aneurysms. Among the 753 patients receiving treatment for spontaneous SAH at our institution, 510 patients fulfilled the inclusion criteria and were thus included in the study (Figure 1). Preoperative variables were reviewed based on medical records, while radiologic findings were recorded by a single neuroradiologist in a blinded fashion.

Definition of Chronic Shunt-Dependent Hydrocephalus
The performance of shunt surgery was, by definition, an essential criterion for defining a chronic shunt-dependent hydrocephalus and was therefore recorded as the target variable in our dataset. Shunt surgery was performed for patients who continued to show ventricle enlargement correlating with relevant symptoms indicating acute or chronic elevated intracranial pressure, for example, mental deterioration, cognitive impairment, and incontinence. This primary outcome informs our predictive modeling and result interpretation throughout the study.

Features and Data Preprocessing
We carefully gathered a variety of features to construct our dataset, which is designed to predict chronic shunt-dependent hydrocephalus in patients with SAH. Our dataset encompasses age at diagnosis and sex, in addition to clinical assessment tools such as the Hunt & Hess grading and the Fisher grading, which measure SAH severity and vasospasm risk, respectively. We also considered medical complications, particularly central nervous system (CNS) infection, and incorporated variables that describe aneurysm characteristics, including its presence and dimensions to outline its size. Treatment methods such as endovascular coiling, surgical clipping, and the placement of external ventricular drainages (EVD) were included in our analysis, offering a detailed view of the factors affecting patient outcomes. The final dataset used for our study consists of records from 510 patients.

Statistical Analysis
We used the Shapiro-Wilk test to assess the normality of the distribution of our data. Depending on the results of this test, we applied either the 2-sample t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test for metric data analysis, appropriate to the data's distribution. For categorical data, the Chi-squared test was used. Statistical significance was established at a 2-sided P value of less than 0.05. Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 28.0 (IBM Corporation, NY, USA).

Predictive Modeling
We applied various ML classifiers, such as decision trees, random forests, gradient boosting trees, logistic regression, support vector machines (both linear and nonlinear), and k-nearest neighbors (KNN), aiming to predict chronic shunt-dependent hydrocephalus in patients with SAH. We trained these models using a repeated cross-validation (CV) strategy with 10 folds and 10 repetitions to facilitate a robust evaluation.
We preprocessed the data to enhance model performance and interpretability. Categorical variables were transformed using one-hot encoding, including the simplification of the aneurysm location variable for patients with diagnosed aneurysms into 2 categories: those located in the anterior communicating artery (ACom) and all others. For a detailed enumeration of the original categories, please see Table 1.
Prior to modeling, each feature underwent standardization by centering and scaling to unit variance, incorporated as a preprocessing step in our pipeline to ensure data scale uniformity across features.
For optimizing hyperparameters, we used nested CV, incorporating either grid search or random search in the inner loop to mitigate hyperparameter selection bias.
To address the imbalance resulting from a higher number of patients without shunt implantation in the target variable distribution, we implemented class weights inversely proportional to class frequencies for all models except KNN. For KNN, we applied the Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique to oversample the minority class where shunt implantation was true, thereby balancing the dataset.
To assess the effectiveness of the trained models, we focused on several metrics: positive predictive value or precision, which gauges the accuracy of positive predictions; negative predictive value (NPV), measuring the accuracy of negative predictions; area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC) and area under the precision-recall curve (AUC-PR), both of which evaluate a model's discriminatory power between classes; balanced accuracy, which averages sensitivity and specificity to reflect overall performance; sensitivity, or the true positive rate (TPR), indicating the model's ability to identify positive cases; and specificity, the true negative rate (TNR), assessing the model's capability to detect negatives. Our evaluation encompassed 510 patient records, with each model's performance quantified and reported through the mean and standard deviation of the metrics across the test folds in our repeated CV analysis.
To generate the precision-recall (PR) and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for each model, we pooled the predictions from all test folds of repeated CV. Subsequently, we calculated the curves using these combined predictions. Additionally, we indicated the mean recall and precision on the PR curve, and the mean false positive rate and TPR on the ROC curve for each model. Please see Figure 2 for the PR plot and Figure 3 for the ROC plot.
We assessed the feature importance in our logistic regression models by interpreting the coefficients and odds ratios (ORs), which provided a quantitative measure of how each predictor influenced the likelihood of chronic shunt-dependent hydrocephalus.
All data processing, modeling, and analysis were conducted using Python 3.11, using libraries such as Scikit-learn, NumPy, and Pandas.

Open Science and Data Accessibility
In our research, both the dataset and the code used to generate the ML models have been made publicly available. The dataset is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) license, and the code is licensed under the Massachusetts Institute of Technology license, both available at https://github.com/RISCSoftware/shuntclf. This open-source approach facilitates transparency and allows for the reproducibility and further exploration of our methods by the wider scientific community.

Interactive Web Application for Model Predictions
Additionally, we have deployed an interactive web interface using Gradio, hosted at https://huggingface.co/spaces/risc42/shuntclf. This interface allows users to input individual patient data and receive predictions on the likelihood of requiring shunt surgery, based on our ML models. In contrast to the models described in the article which used nested CV for robust evaluation, the models available through the interface were trained on the entire dataset. This approach was chosen to optimize the models' performance when predicting new cases in real-time. In the web application, the trained scikit-learn models are preloaded from disk, and the input data from the user are processed to output a binary decision indicating the necessity of shunt surgery (yes or no). In addition, selected models provide the probability and the corresponding odds.
